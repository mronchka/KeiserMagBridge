Keiser M3 Bridge
Keiser M3 Bridge In development

Your Keiser M3, live in Zwift. No wires cut.

A small board that sits on the bike's plastic cover and reads the bike the way its own console does: from magnets. It works out your gear and cadence, calculates your watts with Keiser's own power curve, and sends power and cadence to Zwift over Bluetooth.

  • 0cables spliced or tapped. The Keiser console keeps working.
  • 24Keiser gears, read through the plastic from the resistance magnets.
  • 50sensor readings a second, averaged over each pedal turn.
  1. Reads the gear leverWhen you move the lever, the resistance magnets swing over the flywheel and the magnetic field at the board changes. A 25-point calibration maps that field to each gear on the console.
  2. Counts your pedal turnsThe crank carries a small magnet for the Keiser's own RPM sensor. Each time it passes, the board sees a sharp dip and counts one turn. No separate cadence sensor needed.
  3. Sends watts to ZwiftAveraging each full pedal turn cancels the crank magnet's swing, leaving a steady gear. Gear and cadence go through Keiser's power formula and out as a standard Bluetooth power meter.

Show in the sensor view

Gear path. With the crank parked, sweeping the lever from fully down to fully up moves the reading along this curve. Every gear sits somewhere on it, closest together near the top.

Crank turns. Each pedal turn drags the reading round a loop. The sharp corner (red end of the colour wheel) is the magnet passing closest to the board; that dip is what the board counts as one turn. Three turns are drawn and lie almost on top of each other.

Per-turn averages. Averaging over each full turn lands on the middle of the loop, so the crank's swing cancels. These dots walk up a curve parallel to the gear path as you changed gear, offset by the crank's average field.

Combined reading. The bright marker is gear plus crank: the loop centred on the per-turn track at the lever position, at the crank position you set. Fields add, so this is what the sensor reads at any moment. The bike model is approximate; the sensor's axes are its own and are not aligned to the bike.
